❓ How it works

Load a folder of short sounds (or drop one anywhere). Each subfolder becomes a bank of up to 36 pads, laid out like your keyboard. Hit the keys to play. Everything stays on your computer; nothing is uploaded.

  • Click a pad (or Shift+its key) to edit it: drag the red edges on the waveform to trim it, drag the yellow part to move it along the file (or step it with ← →), pick One-shot, Hold (plays while the key is down) or Loop (hit once to start, again to stop), and set volume, pitch, pan and reverse.
  • Drum machine: click the small dot on a pad to make it an element. Once = pulse (yellow, plays on the beat grid), twice = drift (green, calls out freely, off the beat, a little detuned), three times = off. Then draw a line: each element sits at its own height (its lane), and the closer the line passes to it, the more it plays. Gaps in the line are rests for the pulse; drifting elements keep calling. Life is how busy it gets, Reach how far from the line elements still respond, Organic loosens timing with swing, flams and detune, and Mutate lets the line move a little every loop. You can still play the keys on top. Kits save the drawing too.
  • Choke groups cut each other off: put an open and a closed hi-hat in the same group.
  • 🎲 Re-roll swaps a pad, or the whole bank, for other sounds from the same folder, so the archive keeps surprising you.
  • ✂ Chop cuts a longer sound into 4, 8 or 16 equal parts across the following pads.
  • Drop a single file onto a pad to put it there. Pads keep the first 30 s of a sound (2 minutes when dropped on a pad).
  • Kits remember which sound sits on which key with all its settings. Load the same folder later and recall them.
  • RECORD captures what you play to a WAV file. Works on the keyboard layout you have, Arabic included: it's the key position that counts.
